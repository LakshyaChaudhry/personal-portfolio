I spent the summer in San Jose on Cisco's AI Defense team.

The problem that kept coming up: agents don't fail in a single sentence. They call tools, write memory, talk to other agents, and only later produce something that looks fine. If you only look at the last message, you miss the part that actually went wrong.

<img src="/assets/blog/cisco-office.jpg" alt="Interns in a working session" style={{width: '100%', borderRadius: '0.5rem', margin: '1.5rem 0 2rem'}} />

I worked on two projects.

The first was an architecture for catching that kind of failure, and evaluating whether it actually does. Traces in, a detector, metrics out. I am not going to describe the system. The work was: here is the problem, here is a way to look at it, does it hold up.

The second was a research project from scratch, in the same broader space. Early, ground-up. Same constraint as the first: I can say I did it, not how.

Most of what I am taking with me is not a method. It is the habit of turning a fuzzy "the agent should not do this" into something you can measure.

Lakshya C.
